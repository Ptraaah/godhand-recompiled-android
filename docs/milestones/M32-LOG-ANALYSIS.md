## Verdict first

- L1 (folded-flag fast FMACs) delivered its priced mechanism on the execution layer, and only there: pooled heavy-window ns/vcyc fell 11.45 → **10.28** (−10.2%, inside PATH-THROUGHPUT's predicted −6 to −11% band), and the character-transform program 0x0520 fell 22.0 → **18.05 µs/call** (−18.0%).
- The stage program 0x0358 did not move at all: **22.57 µs/call** pooled vs m31's 22.3 — slightly worse than baseline, far off the ≤20 target. It carries ~40% of listed program time, so roughly half of L1's intended target never received the lever's benefit on device, whatever the fixture rig promised (pooled fixture A/B was −19.6%; the device session realised −10.2% on the run layer and −6.4% on the worker layer).
- The crowd ceiling and the worst join did not move: heavy fields/s ceiling 55.7 (m31: 58.7), floor 41.6 (m31: 47.8), worst join_wait **1,040.9 ms** at matched load (m31: 1,007.4 ms). No heavy window touches 60.
- What changed is the shape of the session: joins became bimodal. Seven of thirteen heavy windows now run with join_wait ≤ 119.5 ms (three at ≤ 15.9 ms — effectively zero), while six windows carry 269.7–1,040.9 ms. The median heavy join collapsed; the worst did not. That is exactly what "better but unstable" feels like from the player's side.
- The callback fixed stack is now the largest *measured* removable share: the vu1worker − vu1 gap widened from m31's 13.8% to **17.1% of worker time** (3.77 µs/run vs 3.23 µs/run), because L1 shrank run cost while the per-run fixed cost stood still. L2 (callback diet) is the m33 lever, priced directly by this log (section: Next lever).

## Stability analysis — the owner's axis

**Heavy fields/s distribution (windows 5–17, n=13).** Min 41.6, median 52.9, mean 51.8, max 55.7, population stdev 3.95 (coefficient of variation 7.6%). Worst five: 41.6, 47.5, 48.3, 50.1, 50.6. The spread is 14.1 fields/s wide — a quarter of the mean — and it is not smooth gradation: adjacent-window swings reach 10.7 fields/s (window 7 → 8: 41.6 → 52.3), 8.7 (6 → 7), 5.4 (16 → 17), 5.1 (14 → 15). A player does not experience the mean of this; he experiences the swings, and the swings are the session.

**Join_wait distribution — bimodal, threshold behaviour.** Heavy windows sorted: 8.6, 11.9, 15.9, 55.8, 57.2, 103.7, 119.5, 269.7, 330.8, 386.3, 472.5, 638.9, 1,040.9 ms. Median 119.5, mean 270.1, total 3,511.7 ms over 8,696 waits. There is a clean gap between 119.5 and 269.7 — no window lives in between. The mechanism reads directly off the pairing with worker load: when the worker finishes the field's runs inside the cadence, the EE's sync touches find completed work and joins collapse to near zero (windows 11, 12, 15: 8.6–15.9 ms); when it does not, every touch pays, waits multiply (up to 2,800 in one window, 13.5 per field in window 7, at 0.37–0.49 ms per wait), and the bill explodes superlinearly. L1 moved more windows under the threshold (m31's heavy joins ran 32.2–1,007.4 with no near-zero windows in its reported range); it did not lower the cost of being above it.

**Per-window variance of the load itself.** VU busy ([ee-work]) across heavy windows: 36–88% (median 57%). Worker ms: 1,695.6–4,198.2 (median 2,711.6 — a 2.5× range). GS idle in the same windows: 59–78% (median 70%) vs m31's 63–72% — the GS still waits on the VU in every heavy window; GIF volume is 248.5–455.3 MB/window over 3,987–9,164 jobs, same regime as m31 (316.2–514.0 MB), and is not the wall while the GS idles at these levels. queue_hw = 64 and queued ≈ run in all 17 windows (one window runs 313 of 314 queued) — no backlog, same as m31.

**Where the bad windows cluster — two distinct mechanisms.**
1. *Fight entry and first crowd (windows 5–7):* fps 48.3 / 50.1 / 41.6; joins 386.3 / 638.9 / 1,040.9; the session's only consume park (window 5), its pipeline park spike (403.8 ms), its callExec spike (1,266.7 ms), and its single heaviest window by every measure (window 7: 202,300 runs — session max — 455.3 MB GIF — session max — worker 4,198.2 ms). Entry burst and peak crowd land together here.
2. *Sustained late crowd (windows 14, 16, 17):* fps 50.6 / 52.9 / 47.5 with worker time pinned at 3,835.8–3,868.8 ms. Window 17 is the instructive one: its join is only 119.5 ms — a "good" join window — yet it is the second-slowest window of the session, because both top programs spike per-call in its profile block (0x0520 22.02 µs/call, 0x0358 27.91 µs/call, both session worsts) and its ns/vcyc (11.73) is the session's worst. That is a *cost-spike* window, not a *sync* window: a different failure mode from window 7, invisible to any analysis that only watches joins. The likely reading is scene mix (a stage-program image mix whose per-call cost is higher), but — as noted under line 2 — the tops do not split by image, so this log cannot prove it.
The good windows (8–12 and 15: fps 52.3–55.7, joins 8.6–103.7) still cap at 55.7. Even when everything goes right, this build's crowd ceiling is ~56, not 60.

**Audio.** The log still carries no SPU/underrun instrumentation; the audio evidence is the field-production texture, exactly as in m31. On that evidence the stutter has **not** decoupled: its predicted worst moments are the same measured events — joins of 386–1,041 ms in six heavy windows (sub-millisecond-to-millisecond hitches, hundreds to thousands per window), the 19.1 ms consume blocks at fight entry, and the 403.8 ms entry pipeline park. In the three near-zero-join windows the texture predicts near-clean audio, but the owner's verdict is session-level and crowds are 13 of this session's 17 windows — the bad texture dominates his sample. If a future verdict reports stutter inside a window the log shows at ≤ ~16 ms join, that would decouple the audio from this mechanism and reopen the question; nothing in this return does.

## Premise verdict — PATH-THROUGHPUT / L1

**Partially delivered.** The mechanism is real and priced correctly at the layer it aimed at: ns/vcyc −10.2% pooled (predicted −6 to −11%), µs/run on the run layer 20.2 → 18.2, and the program whose cost is segment FMACs (0x0520) gained −18.0%, close to its fixture numbers (−27.1 / −30.7% on the rig, always with the in-range-operand caveat). Three things kept the fixture's pooled −19.6% from reaching the device session:

1. 0x0358 — 40% of listed program time — gained nothing on device (+1.2%). The fixture already contained this warning (one image at −5.4%, non-FMAC-dominated); the device mix played the unresponsive half.
2. The fixed callback stack does not shrink when runs get cheaper; it grew to 17.1% of worker time (3.77 µs/run). Of the worker layer's total improvement (23.47 → 21.98 µs/run, −6.4%), the fixed stack clawed back the difference between the run layer's −10.0% and the worker's −6.4%.
3. The bad windows are threshold events, not averages: a ~10% cheaper run does not rescue a window whose worker load is 2.5× the median, so the worst join and the floor — the lines the owner feels — stayed where m31 left them.

The remaining wall, in measured order: per-run fixed callback cost (17.1% of worker, directly removable), 0x0358's unresponsive run cost (22.57 µs/call, needs per-image evidence before it can be aimed at), then the join bill as a *consequence* of worker saturation (not an independent wall — its near-zero windows prove the sync machinery itself is cheap when work is done). GIF volume and the job layer are ruled out by this log's own counters (GS idle 59–78%; drain+publish < 1% of job busy; consumeBytes 0.00 MB).

## Next lever for m33 — L2, the callback diet (one lever, as pre-planned in PATH-THROUGHPUT §L2)

**Scope.** (a) Lean execute: skip the full resetScheduler() (~3.5 KB of memsets per run — at this session's 1.71M heavy-window runs, the traffic is on the order of gigabytes per session) when the previous run ended clean, with a conservative dirty bit set by any budget cut, stop request, interpreter pair, state load, or reset; the VU0 lean precedent (PS2X_VU0_LEAN) and its audit checker port directly — the checker runs in the verify soak. (b) Capture bitset: replace the per-run unordered_map increment in the capture hook with a per-image 256-bit entry bitset indexed by pc>>3; the map is touched only on a bit flip for the once-per-entry file append. Observability-only, byte-identical outputs.

**Kill switch.** PS2X_VU1_LEAN_EXECUTE=0 restores the unconditional resetScheduler per execute, in the same binary, seeded by variant default like every previous stage's switch. The capture half needs no switch (it changes no behaviour).

**Expected gain, grounded in this log.** The prize is measured, not modelled: 6,444 ms of the heavy windows' 37,617 ms worker time (17.1%, 3.77 µs/run). PATH-THROUGHPUT priced recovery at 40–70% of the gap; applied to this session's numbers that is worker busy −6.8% to −12.0% in heavy windows, worker µs/run 21.98 → ~19.4–20.5, and the gap signature narrowing from 17.1% toward ~5–10%. Joins should fall in proportion in mid windows (more windows pushed under the threshold), and the worst join at matched load is expected to ease toward ~920–970 ms — **not** below it by this lever alone, and fields/s expectations stay honest: +1–3 in mid crowds, ceiling perhaps 57–59, no promise of 60, because 0x0358's run cost is untouched by L2. The arbitration signature is precise: ns/vcyc must stay at ~10.28 (L2 does not touch run cost — if it moves, attribution is broken) while the gap narrows.

**Risk signature.** This is the risk-bearing lever of the three, and its failure mode is worse than L1's: if clean-end detection misses a path that leaves scheduler state behind, the next program reads stale ready-times or flag records — too-early reads, wrong values, arbitrary and potentially intermittent visual corruption, scene-specific rather than a clean sign-of-zero class. The mitigations are the design's own: conservative dirty bit, the ported audit checker in soak, and the kill switch for a same-binary device A/B. A geometry defect appearing only with the lean path on is this lever's automatic fail line.

**Explicitly not recommended.** Join-frequency reduction (the near-zero join windows prove sync is cheap when work completes; the bill is real unfinished work, and m30 already bounded these sites — this log does not overturn that), a second VU worker (queue_hw and queued ≈ run show no backlog to parallelise; the wall is inside execution), and skipping loads/vnorm (untouched by L1 and by this recommendation; the round-9 rule stands). One instrumentation ask rides with m33, cheap and decisive for the stage after: split [ee-prof:top] by image hash, so 0x0358's two copies stop merging into one pooled figure and its cost can be aimed at with evidence instead of inference.

## Standing

- m32 is a real step over m31 on the average window (ns/vcyc −10.2%, 0x0520 −18.0%, median heavy join 119.5 ms with three near-zero windows, entry consume down from 418.7 ms across two windows to 114.3 ms in one) and no step at all on the lines the owner weights most: crowd ceiling, floor, worst join, and the audio texture that follows them. His verdict and the log are the same report in two vocabularies.
- m27 remains the playable fallback anchor by the standing rule until a successor is declared. On this evidence m32 is the strongest bridge-path build measured, and the working base for m33 — with the declaration left, as always, to the owner's feel.
