## The crowd wall, profiled for the throughput stage

All figures from the 34 heavy windows (VU ≥34%) unless noted; this section is written to be cited standalone by the next stage's design doc.

- **Where the time actually is.** The VU1 worker thread executes 80,680-212,711 compiled microprogram runs per 5 s window, totalling 1,593-4,257 ms of worker time per window ([ee-prof] vu1worker) — in the worst windows the single worker is busy ~85% of wall time. The EE submits only 358-492 VU jobs/window ([vu1] queued; queued ≈ run every window, no backlog growth) and 5,180-9,833 GIF jobs/window carrying 316.2-514.0 MB/window ([ee-work]; ~1.3-2.1 MB per field, peak 2.12). VU busy fraction ([ee-work]): 34-89% across heavy windows, 50-89% in the VU≥50% subset. Meanwhile the GS worker is 63-72% **idle** in those same windows and the bridge pipeline parks ~0.0 ms in steady state (nonzero parks: 36.6-361.1 ms, all in boot/entry windows). Every downstream consumer has slack; the producer — VU1 execution — does not.
- **The join bill and its split.** join_wait in heavy windows: 32.2-1,007.4 ms. Two buckets are ~95%+ of it in every heavy window; everything else is noise (regread = 0.0 ms in all 37 windows — the m28 lever still perfect; regwrite ≤3.0; gspriv ≤9.7; vumem/download/vupoll/backpressure ~0):
  - `barrier=` (the m30 bounded-staleness vblank barrier): max 508.0 ms (w30, 1,558 waits), 492.4 ms (w10, 1,569 waits);
  - `idle=` (waitPipelineIdle remnant): max 511.9 ms (w10, 782 waits), 328.0 ms (w32, 523 waits).
  Worst window overall (w10: VU 89%, 50.6 fields/s, GIF 487.8 MB): join 1,007.4 ms over 2,353 waits = barrier 492.4 + idle 511.9 + gspriv 3.1. The waits are individually small (~0.31 ms per barrier wait in w10) but arrive 1,100-2,353 times per bad window — the EE synchronizes with VU1 completion roughly 7-9 times per field in crowds, and each touch finds real unfinished work. This is the m30 conclusion with better instruments: the bound converted semantics into work, and the work is the wall.
- **Queue depth is not the wall.** queue_hw = 64 in all 37 windows (the real cap, not the m27-era fingerprint of 2), and queued ≈ run in every window: the async queue drains as fast as it fills on average; the worker is throughput-bound inside job execution, not starved or flooded.
- **Audio-relevant stall pattern.** The log carries no SPU/underrun instrumentation; the audio evidence is the field-production texture the SPU2 slave clock must follow. Three stall classes, in increasing audibility:
  1. *Cadence jitter (continuous in crowds):* adjacent heavy windows swing 47.8-58.7 fields/s, and within bad windows the EE drops 0.8-1.0 s per 5 s into ~1,100-2,350 small join waits — a sub-millisecond-to-millisecond hitch every 2-4 fields. This is the background "sounds laggie" texture.
  2. *Scene-entry blocks (rare, longest single stalls):* `parked: consume` appears in exactly two windows — the fight-entry pair — at 153.4 ms/6 waits and 265.3 ms/9 waits, i.e. individual EE blocks of ~25-30 ms, roughly 1.5 field periods each. These are the single most audible events in the session.
  3. *Ruled out:* `[ee-work] queueWait max 909.8 ms` appears identically in all 37 windows of this session — it is a per-session cumulative maximum set during boot/first-field drain (every session in the appended log carries its own constant fossil: 556.5-1,667.8 ms), never grows during play, and queueWait avg during play is 0.23-1.41 ms (5.61/4.31 only in the two entry windows). It is not a gameplay stall and no stage should be aimed at it.
- **Frame geometry (background).** Session present-rects span the same modes as m30 (640×896 to 1280×960 at scale 2, 2.3-4.9 MB frames) — line 1's halving happened at constant frame size, so it is the copy count, not a smaller frame, that paid.

## What the throughput stage should attack, ranked by these numbers

1. **Per-run VU1 execution cost on the worker.** The only lever that raises the ceiling: the worker's 1,593-4,257 ms/window of compiled-microprogram execution is what every join waits for, while GS, bridge and host all idle. The 27 vu-capture bins returned with this very log (m31-device-return/files/vu-capture/, with entries.txt) are the crowd programs' AOT inputs — profile which captures dominate crowd windows and cut their per-run cost first.
2. **Join frequency at the barrier/idle sites.** 1,100-2,353 synchronization touches per bad window at ~0.3 ms each is a real tax stacked on top of the work itself; coarser completion granularity (fewer, larger sync points per field) attacks the EE-side bill without touching execution. Ranked second because m30 already bounded these sites once — what remains is backed by real work, so the recoverable share is the per-touch overhead, capped by target 1.
3. **Scene-entry smoothness (the consume-park class).** ~25-30 ms individual blocks at fight entry are the most audible single defect. Likely first-burst costs (cold microprogram compiles / first-touch data) landing on the entry fields; a warm-up or prefetch at load time would move them off the audible path. Small in total milliseconds, disproportionate in what the owner hears.
4. **GIF volume (514 MB/window peak) — only via targets 1-2.** Upload dedup already exists (ARUS pillar 1); the remaining volume is geometry streaming that exists to feed VU1. It is not an independent wall while the GS idles at 63-72%.

Explicitly closed by this log: the present path (targets met, do not re-aim), pipeline depth (park ~0, dropped 0), regread (0.0 in 37/37), renderer seed and variant integrity (fired correctly again).

## Standing

- m30's stage (PATH-VBLANK) and m31's stage (PATH-PRESENT) are both closed by device evidence: each lever fired, each landed inside its predicted band, and each time the wall moved to the next real thing rather than shrinking cosmetically.
- What remains is one wall with three faces: VU1 per-run execution cost (the ceiling), join frequency (the tax on touching it), and scene-entry first-burst stalls (the loudest single symptom). The audio follows all three and needs no stage of its own.
- m27 (0.28-m27-pipeline, versionCode 35) remains the playable fallback anchor until a successor beats it on the device by the standing rule. m31's log makes it the strongest bridge-path build measured so far; the declaration is the owner's, and his verdict ("getting better and better... playable as not playable") is consistent with promoting m31 to the working base for the throughput stage while keeping m27 as the retreat line.
